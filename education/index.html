Urology education

Clear, organized overviews of common urology conditions, how they are diagnosed, and how they are managed.

For education only. These guides do not replace advice from your own care team.

  • Male pelvic pain

    Chronic Pelvic Pain Syndrome (CPPS) is persistent pain or discomfort in the pelvic region without a clear infection or structural cause. It is often linked with urinary symptoms, sexual dysfunction and pelvic floor muscle tension. Management usually combines medical, physical and behavioural approaches.

  • Urinary tract infections

    UTIs happen when bacteria enter and multiply in the urinary system. They often cause burning with urination, frequency or urgency, and lower abdominal discomfort. Diagnosis is based on symptoms and urine testing, and treatment usually involves antibiotics and supportive care.

  • Kegel exercises

    Kegel exercise training for men and women, used to treat a range of conditions that affect urinary function.

  • Cystectomy prehabilitation

    Prehabilitation prepares your body and mind for radical cystectomy through exercise, nutrition, smoking cessation and psychological readiness. Patients who take part tend to have fewer complications, shorter hospital stays and faster recovery.

  • Prostate cancer treatment decision aid

    Compares active surveillance, surgery and radiation therapy, with the benefits, risks and side effects of each, to help you choose the treatment that fits your values and circumstances.

  • PSA decision aid: biopsy, MRI, or what?

    For patients with an elevated PSA. Lays out the options for further evaluation, including prostate MRI, biopsy or continued monitoring, and the benefits, risks and uncertainties of each.

  • Intravesical treatments

    Intravesical therapy places medication directly into the bladder through a catheter to treat certain bladder cancers, often after tumour removal to lower the risk of recurrence. Covers how treatment is given, preparation, aftercare and safety precautions.
